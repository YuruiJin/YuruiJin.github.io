## Why I started watching WhynotTV systematically

I recently started watching WhynotTV in a more systematic way. For me, its value is not only in learning about individual guests and their life stories, but also in observing the growth patterns behind people who have built meaningful careers.

I am currently a Robotics MSc student and I am preparing for the next stage: applying for and entering PhD-level research. At this point, improving my technical skills is necessary, but it is not enough. I also need to think about a longer-term question:

> How should someone doing robotics research build a personal system for growth?

## I do not want to remain a passive content consumer

A lot of content disappears from memory soon after we watch it. The more valuable practice is to convert content into a framework for judgment, decision-making, and action.

For this reason, I plan to read each episode through three layers:

1. The guest's experience: what this person actually went through;
2. The growth model: what reusable patterns can be found behind that experience;
3. My own application: what this means for my research, PhD preparation, and long-term capability building.

This changes WhynotTV from entertainment into a source of structured reflection. The point is not to summarize every detail, but to extract models that can shape how I learn, work, and present myself as an early-stage researcher.

## What I will focus on

In the following notes, I will pay particular attention to several questions:

- How do technical people find and refine a long-term direction?
- How can learning, projects, papers, and writing become long-term assets rather than isolated tasks?
- How can public output become part of a personal research portfolio?
- How does one move from simply completing assigned tasks to defining meaningful problems?
- How can daily input be transformed into research output?

These questions matter to me because robotics research requires more than technical execution. It also requires taste, persistence, communication, and the ability to build a coherent identity around the problems one chooses to study.

## How this relates to my current stage

For me, this series is not a set of casual viewing notes. It is a form of self-training before the PhD stage.

I want these notes to help me gradually answer three questions:

- What kind of robotics researcher do I want to become?
- How should I build my long-term research capabilities?
- How can my GitHub Pages site evolve from a personal homepage into a public archive of research growth?

This is also part of learning how to think in public. By turning interviews into written reflections, I can practice connecting other people's experiences with my own path, while making my learning process visible and reusable.

## Next steps

I will continue turning selected WhynotTV episodes into short public notes. Each note will not aim to reproduce the full conversation. Instead, it will focus on one core model and map that model onto my own path in robotics research, PhD preparation, and long-term output building.
